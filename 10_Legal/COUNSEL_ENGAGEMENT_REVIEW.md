## 4. What this actually costs — the headline is not the number

v2 §4.1 calls KES 259,250 an **"all-inclusive legal fee"**. Four clauses later, §4.5 says: *"We will also charge to you any additional disbursements and other expenses we incur on your behalf. VAT will be charged to you in addition where applicable."* **The two statements cannot both be true.** Three things sit outside the headline:

| Item | Basis | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **VAT** | Terms cl 6.1: fees exclusive of VAT. cl 6.2: disbursements may attract it. Kenya VAT = 16% | **+~KES 29,600** on fees, plus VAT on firm-side expenses |
| **Withholding gross-up** | v2 §4.3 / v1 §5.4 — if WHT is deducted, the payment *increases* so the firm nets the full fee | If 5% WHT on professional fees applies: **+~KES 9,737**. **This clause makes WHT the client's cost, not a credit against the firm's tax** |
| **Stamp duty on nominal share capital** | Not in v2's table at all. v1 §4.3 said it *"depend[s] on the nominal share capital"* and would be confirmed later. **Nominal capital is not yet set** — v2 §2.1.1 is the advice that sets it | **Unknowable today.** This repo's desk research says 1% of nominal capital |

**Realistic envelope, if the firm is VAT-registered and WHT applies:** roughly **KES 295,000–305,000**, against a headline of 259,250 — before stamp duty and before any trademark class beyond the first.

**Three questions follow, and they are cheap to ask:** Is the firm VAT-registered (cl 6.1 promises invoices state VAT)? Does WHT apply to a payment made by an individual pre-incorporation, and will the firm accept bearing it rather than grossing up? Is stamp duty on nominal capital still chargeable in Kenya, and at what rate?

| Application charges | 6,000 | 5,000 |
| Advertisement in journal | 4,500 | 3,000 |
| Certificates of registration | 2,000 | 3,000 |
| E-citizen registration | 7,050 | — |
| Appointment of agent | 2,500 | — |
| Stamping charges | 3,000 | — |
| Photocopies & petties | 4,500 | — |
| **Registry disbursements & costs & fast tracking** | **12,450** | — |
| **Total** | **50,000** | **~13,000** |

Two observations, offered as questions rather than accusations. **The 12,450 "registry disbursements & costs & fast tracking" line is undescribed and is 25% of the block** — and it is exactly the figure that brings 37,550 to a round 50,000. And per [`LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md`](LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md) §4.3, trademark registration runs **8–18 months regardless**; it is worth asking what "fast tracking" buys.

Separately, several lines here are the **firm's own expenses** (photocopies & petties, appointment of agent), which Terms cl 5.3 classifies as *expenses*, not *disbursements* — while §4.5 reserves the right to charge further expenses on top.

## 5. 🔴 Where the IP lands — the most expensive line in either letter

This is the one item where the two letters differ in a way that has a direct, quantifiable cost.

| | v1 (29 Jul) | v2 (6 Aug) | Owner's stated intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trademark registered to | **The Company** (2.1.8) | **Mary personally** — *"in your favour"* (2.1.7) | **The holding company** |
| Copyright registered to | Not specified; conditional on advice (2.1.7) | **Mary personally** — *"in your favour"* (2.1.6) | **The holding company** |

[`LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md`](LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md) §1 records the owner's intent as *"all IP ownership — not only trademark and copyright — to sit at holding-company level"*, and §4.1 records why the first registration is the expensive decision: moving IP between entities later attracts **capital gains tax at 5% where value has accrued, stamp duty on the deed of assignment, and withholding tax on royalties** paid upward.

**Neither letter puts the IP where the owner intends it.** v1 puts it one step away (Company → holdco: one taxable transfer). **v2 puts it two steps away** (Mary → Company → holdco: potentially two).

**But v1 also contains the tension that has to be resolved before anything is filed.** v1 §1.3 commits to advising on *"the correct sequencing of incorporation between the two entities"* — yet v1 §2.1.1 incorporates the operating Company **now**, as Phase 1 priority. **The sequencing advice is promised for after the sequencing decision has been executed.** That is precisely the ordering [`LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md`](LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md) §4.1 identifies as converting a free decision into a taxable one.

**The resolution is not to stop incorporating.** Trading readiness is the priority and incorporation is not the problem. **The resolution is to decouple: incorporate the operating Company now, and file nothing at KIPI or KECOBO until the holdco question is answered.** v1's phasing already permits this — Phase 2 streams are invoiced *"when you instruct us to commence"*. v2's phasing does not exist, so nothing holds the IP filings back.

**⚠️ Note the tax scope-out.** Terms cl 4.4: *"the scope of our services will not include advice on tax issues (including the tax consequences of transactions or other legal arrangements)"* unless expressly agreed. **The IP-location question is substantially a tax question.** Unless the engagement letter expressly brings it in scope, the firm is not contracted to answer the part that costs the money. This needs to be expressly agreed in writing, or an accountant instructed alongside — as [`LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md`](LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md) §4.1 already flags.

## 6. 🔴 The copyright advice was the most valuable thing in v1, and v2 removed it

**v1 §2.1.6 is a direct, competent answer to the hardest question this repository has raised.** It commits to advising on what subsists in AI-assisted work, who is the author, and what records evidence sufficient human authorship — *"having regard to the reasoning in Aryeh Movement Limited v. Cynthia Beldina Akoth Okello (COPTA/E001/2025) … and to the version-controlled record of direction, specification and revision that you have indicated you can produce."*

**Two things that clause confirms, and both should be recorded as findings:**

1. **Counsel has engaged with COPTA/E001/2025 substantively** — it is cited by name, and the human-authorship point is stated correctly and unprompted.
2. **Counsel has accepted this repository's commit history as the evidentiary record.** That independently validates [`LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md`](LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md) §4.2's conclusion — *"the mitigation is record-keeping, and it should start now… its evidentiary value is a reason to keep maintaining them."* **A lawyer has now said the same thing. Keep maintaining the decision logs and changelogs.**

**v1 also got the gating right.** §2.1.6 advises; §2.1.7 files *"if, following that advice, you instruct us to do so."* Advice first, registration second — exactly [`LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md`](LEGAL_ENTITY_SETUP.md) §5 Phase 3 item 11.

**v2 inverts all of it.** §2.1.5 offers advice on *the registration process*; §2.1.3 offers generic advice on *"the nature and essence of copyrights and trademarks"*; and §2.1.6 registers the copyright **unconditionally**. The case is not mentioned. The authorship question is not asked. **The KES 35,000 buys a filing rather than an answer** — for a registration that both letters and this repo agree is **optional and evidentiary, not constitutive**.

**This is the single strongest reason to ask for v1's scope back.**

## 8. Terms of Business — identical in both letters, and unchanged since 29 July

These points attach to the Terms, not to either letter's scope, so agreeing v1's scope does not fix them.

**Drafting defects:**

- 🔴 **"Association Entity" and "Association Person" are never defined.** Clause 1.1's definition list contains five terms and neither of these — yet the entire liability architecture at **cl 17.3, 17.4, 17.5, 19, 20.3, 21.2, 24.3, 26.2 and 26.3** depends on them, as does cl 1.4 and cl 13.2.
